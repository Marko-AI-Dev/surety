### Rules for the edit

- **Real screens only in scenes 4 to 8 and 10.** Record the app; do not mock it. The recorded runs keep their "Recorded" badge on screen.
- **Scene 9 is the only scene that shows something not built,** and it says so on screen for its whole length.
- **Hold on the ledger page.** It is the proof. Two seconds of silence over it is worth more than a sentence.
- **Say "test dollars" once.** The "Testnet. No real money." line stays in the last frame.
- **No market figures in the film.** They belong in the pitch, where you can name the source.
- **Never show a token, a `.env` file or a terminal with secrets.**

## The hardest questions, with short honest answers

1. **Who holds the buyer's key? You do. That is custody.**
   "In the demo our signer holds the buyer's key. In the product the key stays with the customer, or several keys must agree. We sell the brain, not hold the money."

2. **How is this different from XRPL's own escrow?**
   "XRPL's escrow is a lock with a timer; it can't know whether goods shipped or documents match. Surety is the brain on top: the checks, the limits, the second key and the agent team."

3. **The inspector is your friend with a phone. Why would I trust that?**
   "Today it is a stand-in, and we say so. In real trade it is a freight forwarder, an inspection company or the carrier's own signed shipping record. What the demo proves is the mechanism: release needs a key that no agent and no supplier holds."

4. **What stops the supplier and the inspector colluding?**
   "Nothing in software stops two people lying together. It raises the cost: the supplier now needs an accomplice the buyer chose. The stronger answer is the carrier's own signed record as the second key, which is on the roadmap."

5. **Why not RLUSD?**
   "RLUSD's testnet issuer has escrow switched off, so we issue a test dollar with the same trust-line mechanism. Moving to RLUSD is a change of issuer, not of code. We have not run it on RLUSD."

6. **Your bouncer uses a model. Models can be fooled.**
   "Yes. So the model is the second line, not the first. Code rules catch wallets and bank details before any model is asked, and a supplier is out after one red card or two yellows. And a fooled agent still cannot move money: the limits and approved wallets sit with the signer, outside every agent. Our own review found false red cards and fixed them; it is in the repo."

7. **Is the negotiation real? The suppliers are your own bots.**
   "The buyer's agents are real and unscripted. The suppliers in the demo are agents with price floors set by code, so the run fits in minutes. No real supplier has used it yet."

8. **Has a customer seen this?**
   "[Honest answer. If you made the call: who, and what they said. If not: 'Not yet. It is the first thing I am doing after this.']"

9. **What happens when the agents make a bad choice inside the limits?**
   "They can. The Lead once chose a quote five dollars dearer because it was a week faster, and it had to give its reason, which is recorded next to the quote Surety ranked first. In the product the buyer gives the final yes."

10. **How do you make money?**
    "A fee on each settled purchase, charged to the buyer, set well below a letter of credit. [Your number.] It is a proposal; nobody has paid it."

11. **What is actually built, and what is a slide?**
    "Built and run on testnet: the agent negotiation, the message screening, the limits in an isolated signer, the escrow, the inspector's second key, automatic payment and refund, and this screen. Not built: the research agent, onboarding for a new category, and negotiation over days."

12. **Is it legal to do this with real money?**
    "Not researched. Holding and moving client funds across borders touches exchange control and payments licensing. That is why the product keeps the key with the customer."
